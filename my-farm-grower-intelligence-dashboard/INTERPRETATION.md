## Key Patterns

**1. Continuous corn dominates, with one clear rotation break.**
All three fields are planted in corn for the 2025 season. Looking back across
2021–2025, most fields alternate between corn and soybeans on a roughly
annual cycle — a standard corn-soy rotation. The exception is field
osm-984791369, the operation's largest field at 245 acres, which has stayed
in continuous corn through 2024 and 2025 rather than rotating back to
soybeans. Because this is also the largest field on the farm, that
monoculture stretch carries more agronomic weight than a small field would:
continuous corn without a break crop is associated with higher nitrogen
demand, greater disease/pest pressure (e.g., corn rootworm), and gradual
declines in soil organic matter over time if not offset by other
conservation practices.

**2. Soil health is inversely related to field size here.**
The smallest field (2.7 acres) has the highest Soil Health Index (0.869),
while the largest field (245 acres) sits in the middle (0.759), and the
mid-sized field (119 acres) has the lowest score (0.673). This isn't a
causal relationship — acreage doesn't drive soil health — but it's worth
noting because it means the operation's largest and most rotation-stressed
field (osm-984791369) is not compensating with the best soil conditions.
That combination (continuous corn + middling soil health, on the field with
the most acres) is the one part of this operation most worth a closer look
by an agronomist.

**3. Vegetation health (NDVI) is consistent but middling across all three fields.**
Peak-season NDVI values cluster tightly between 0.57 and 0.63 — none of the
three fields stands out as either a clear top performer or a stress outlier.
This is a reasonably healthy but unremarkable vegetation signal; it doesn't
independently confirm or contradict the soil health findings, but it gives
no reason for immediate concern on any single field.

## What This Suggests for Decision-Making

- **Priority for follow-up:** osm-984791369 — largest field, continuous
  corn since 2024, and only mid-range soil health. A grower or agronomist
  reviewing this dashboard should consider whether reintroducing soybeans
  (or another break crop) in the next planting cycle would help offset the
  soil and pest-pressure risks of extended monoculture.
- **Lowest-risk field:** osm-660987142 — smallest acreage, but the
  healthiest soil by a clear margin, and it still fits the corn-soy
  rotation pattern rather than breaking from it.
- **Overall operation read:** a small (367-acre), corn-soybean row crop
  operation with mostly healthy rotation practices, one notable exception
  worth monitoring, and no acute vegetation-health red flags this season.
